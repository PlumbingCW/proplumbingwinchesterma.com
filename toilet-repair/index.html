Toilet overflow emergency: stop the water first

The sequence is simple and it works in under a minute. Take the tank lid off. Push the flapper down to close it, or lift the float arm. Then turn the shutoff valve behind or beside the toilet clockwise. If the valve is stuck, find the main shutoff for the house.

Do not flush again to see if it clears. Each flush adds more water to the bowl. Use a flange plunger, which has a rubber extension that seals the drain opening, and push slowly. If a plunger does not clear it, the blockage may be past the toilet. When more than one fixture is affected, the drain cleaning page explains why.

Running toilet: the quiet water waster

A toilet that refills every few minutes or hisses all night is leaking from the tank into the bowl. The usual causes are a worn flapper that no longer seals, a flush valve that is corroded at its seat or a fill valve that does not shut off. Parts are inexpensive, but the leak can add thousands of gallons a month.

Dye test it by placing a drop of food coloring in the tank, waiting 20 minutes, and checking the bowl. Color means the flapper leaks. A professional leak detection visit is useful if you suspect water loss and the toilet seems fine.

Leak at the base and wobbling toilets

Water around the base of a toilet means the wax ring or the closet bolts have failed, or the flange under the toilet is cracked or sitting too low. A wobbling toilet puts constant pressure on the seal. The fix is to pull the toilet, inspect the flange and set a new wax ring or a modern gasket.

If the flange is rusted or the floor beneath is soft, repair comes before the toilet goes back. Rotted subfloor beneath a bathroom is common in older Winchester houses, where a slow leak may have run for years. A flange repair ring or a section of new subfloor restores a solid mount. Toilet installation and replacement

A new toilet is a good upgrade when the old one is cracked, constantly clogs or wastes water. Modern high-efficiency toilets use about 1.28 gallons per flush or less, which saves water on every use. The plumber sets the new toilet on a fresh ring, connects a new supply line and checks the flush and the seal.

Check the rough-in distance, which is the measure from the wall to the center of the drain, before you buy. Most are 12 inches, but older houses in Winchester may have 10 or 14. Bring the number to the store or ask the plumber. A taller comfort-height bowl and an elongated seat are worth considering for adults.

When a clog is not the toilet's fault

Persistent clogs at a single toilet often come from a worn bowl, but if toilets on the same floor gurgle together the problem is in the branch or the main. Wipes and paper towels flushed down are a top cause. A plumber may use a toilet auger, which works through the bowl without scratching the porcelain, and a camera for repeat cases.

If you are in a rental, tell the dispatcher you are the manager or tenant, who is at the unit and when access is available. Keep the invoice for the landlord or the building record.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts falls under the state plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00, and plumbers are licensed through the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. Ask any plumber to confirm their license and which permit the job needs before work starts.
