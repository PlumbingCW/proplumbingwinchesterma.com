When a hidden leak is an emergency

Some leaks are slow and can wait for a scheduled visit. Others cannot. A ceiling that is dripping, a wall that is warm and wet to the touch, or water pooling around the base of the water heater needs a same-day response. These signs should move you to the phone:

  • Water dripping from a ceiling, light fixture or wall
  • The meter dial moves when every fixture and appliance is off
  • A warm spot on the floor or the sound of running water you cannot trace
  • Sudden low pressure with no work done on the house

Shut off the main valve if water is flowing, and switch off power to the affected area if it is safe. If water is dripping through a fixture on the ceiling, stay away from it. The 24/7 line can dispatch the same day when appointments are available.

Meter still running, or the bill went up

The water meter is the easiest leak detector in the house. Turn off every tap and appliance, then watch the small leak indicator or the sweep hand on the meter. If it moves, water is going somewhere. A toilet that runs silently, an irrigation valve, or a pipe in a wall can all do it.

Every gallon that passes the meter is billed, so a leak shows up on the statement. Start with the quick check: add dye tablets to each toilet tank and wait 20 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a flapper leak, and the toilet repair page covers the fix. If the toilets are fine and the meter still moves, the leak is in a pipe, and a plumber with detection tools is the next step.

How leaks are found without tearing things open

Detection is a process of elimination. The plumber pressure tests sections of pipe to find which zone loses pressure, then uses listening equipment on that zone. Acoustic microphones pick up the sound of water escaping, and thermal cameras show temperature differences where hot water leaks warm a floor or wall.

Moisture meters confirm the wet area and tracer gas is used for hard cases. Each tool narrows the search so the repair opens a small area, not a whole room. Ask which tools will be used before the visit and whether a written finding comes with it. That is helpful when you file a claim.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks

A stain on a ceiling is where water arrives, not where it starts. Water follows framing and pipe runs before it shows, and the real source is often a bathroom above or a supply line in the wall. Roof leaks produce similar stains after a storm. A plumber checks whether the stain tracks to a pipe or to rain, which spares you from fixing the wrong thing.

When the cause is a supply line or drain above, the repair belongs to burst pipe repair or a fixture fix. When it is outside plumbing, the plumber can tell you and point you to a roofer. Detection visits are also offered in Lexington, MA and the other towns around Winchester.

Crawlspace, slab and buried line leaks

Most Winchester houses have basements, but additions, garages and a few slab-on-grade homes have concrete under the floor. A warm spot on the floor, water seen at the edge of a slab, or sound of running water under concrete all point to a slab leak. Hot lines laid in or under concrete are the usual suspect.

Buried lines are also a concern between the street and the house. A damp patch on the lawn, a spongy section of a driveway or a sudden drop in pressure suggests the service line. If that is the finding, the next step is the water service line repair and replacement page.

Irrigation, pool and meter box leaks

Lawn irrigation lines run underground and fail at valves and joints. A zone that stays soggy or a sprinkler that never fully shuts off often signals one. Pool and spa plumbing loses water through unions and returns. Meter box leaks show up as water around the lid at the curb, and the town water department handles the street side while you handle the house side.

Landlords and commercial properties

Property managers often need leak detection that avoids disturbing tenants. Tell the dispatcher the unit number, whether the leak crosses units and what time you can give access. Commercial buildings also need leak detection after hours, and the commercial plumbing page covers what to have ready when you call.

What to do while you wait for leak detection

  • Shut off the main water valve if water is actively escaping and you can reach it safely
  • Do not touch electrical outlets, panels or cords near wet floors or walls
  • Photograph the wet area and note the water meter reading and the time
  • Move valuables and rugs away from damp spots
  • Keep a bucket or towels under any active drip

The rules behind this kind of work come from the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00. Plumbers hold licenses issued by the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, so it is reasonable to ask for a license number and a written scope before any work begins.
