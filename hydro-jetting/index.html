Hydro jetting emergency: when a backup comes back

A line that was snaked last month and is backing up again is the classic jetting situation. The cable punched a hole in the blockage, water drained for a while, and the coating around the hole slowly closed back in. When the backup is spreading to a tub, a floor drain or a laundry standing pipe, waiting only adds cleanup.

Do not run the washer, dishwasher or showers while you wait. Move anything valuable off a basement floor and open a window if there is a sewer odor. If water is near an outlet or the panel, stay out of that area and say so on the call.

How hydro jetting works

A jetting machine feeds a flexible hose with a nozzle into the line through a cleanout. Water at high pressure shoots out through angled jets, which pull the nozzle forward and scour the pipe wall in a spray pattern. The debris flushes downstream with the water instead of being pushed ahead of a cable.

Nozzles come in different styles. A penetrating nozzle opens a stoppage, a rotating or sweeping nozzle scrubs the wall, and a root cutting head trims growth at the joints. The plumber picks one based on what the line holds, then runs a camera afterward when needed to confirm the pipe is clear and to see whether anything else is wrong.

Roots, grease and scale in older Winchester lines

Winchester is a mature town with established yards and street trees. Roots look for moisture and enter at the joints of clay and old cast iron, where they grow into a mat that catches paper. A cable cuts a channel through the mat. A root cutting head and jet can open the full diameter of the pipe, which gives a longer gap before the next clog.

The town center and the Main Street corridor also have many restaurants and commercial buildings, where grease is a regular offender. Commercial kitchens and apartments over storefronts tie into lines that carry heavy grease loads. If you manage such a property, see commercial plumbing in Winchester for grease trap and after-hours service.

When jetting is not the right call

Honest plumbers decline to jet some pipes. A cracked clay line, a collapsed section or a badly corroded cast iron stack can be damaged by pressure, and flooding a broken pipe in the ground makes a wet yard. That is the reason for a camera check first on any line that is very old or has a history of breaks.

If the camera shows a break or a belly in the pipe, the answer becomes repair, not cleaning. The sewer line repair page explains spot repair, relining and replacement so you can compare options before deciding. Homeowners in neighboring Woburn can use the same dispatch line for jetting.

Keeping a jetted line clean

A clean pipe stays clean longer when the habits change. Wipe grease from pans into the trash, not the sink. Use a hair catcher in the shower. Keep wipes out of toilets even when the package says flushable. If roots were the cause, plan a maintenance jet or camera check on a schedule the plumber suggests, since roots regrow.

For homes with a history of backups, ask about a cleanout. A cleanout fitting gives access without removing a toilet, and it makes any future cleaning quicker and cheaper. It also gives your plumber a clean path for a camera.

In Massachusetts, the state plumbing code at 248 CMR 10.00 sets the rules for installation and repair, and licensing comes from the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Winchester Building Department or your plumber what applies to yours.
