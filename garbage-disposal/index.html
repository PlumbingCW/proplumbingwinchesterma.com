Garbage disposal emergency: leaks, backups and power problems

A disposal sits above the drain, below the sink and next to the electrical supply. That is a risky mix when something fails. Switch it off at the wall switch and the breaker before you do anything else. Never reach into the grinding chamber, even if the power is off. Use tongs or pliers.

  • Water dripping from the bottom or the side of the disposal
  • Sink that fills with gray water when the dishwasher runs
  • Humming without grinding, a sign of a jam
  • Breaker that trips when the unit starts

Dripping at the sides usually means a failed seal or a cracked housing, and replacement is the answer. Dripping at the top flange or the drain connections can sometimes be fixed with putty or new gaskets.

Jams and hums: what you can safely try

Most disposals have a hex socket in the bottom center. With the power off, you can insert the matching wrench and work it back and forth to free the flywheel. After that, press the red reset button on the base. If it trips again immediately, the motor or wiring has a fault.

Do not use a drain cleaner in a jammed disposal and do not run the unit for long if it hums. A disposal that will not turn at all can have a seized motor. If the jam comes with a sink that will not drain, the blockage may be in the line, and the drain cleaning page covers the clearing.

Most disposals last about eight to twelve years. If yours is past that and leaking from the housing, put the money toward a new unit. Check the horsepower: one-third is enough for light use, and a half or three-quarters suits a family kitchen.

Installing a garbage disposal on a double sink

A double bowl sink has two drains that join at a tee before the trap. The disposal goes on one drain, usually the deeper bowl, and a discharge tube connects it to the tee. The other bowl's drain ties into the disposal through the same assembly, so it can feed through it. The plumber needs the right tube length and slope to prevent backups.

Dishwasher drain connection is the other detail. The dishwasher hose connects to a port on the disposal, and a knockout plug in that port has to be removed or the dishwasher will not drain. Many do-it-yourself jobs forget this. A properly installed dishwasher drain loops high to prevent backflow. Under-sink space in older kitchens is tight, so measure the cabinet. Disposal installs are also scheduled in the Arlington service area, where kitchens in older multi-family homes are often compact.

Electrical and code points

A disposal needs a switched outlet or a hardwired connection. If the sink base has no outlet, a licensed electrician may be needed to add one. Massachusetts guidance indicates replacing fixtures can require a plumbing permit. Ask the plumber about the permit for new work. Replacing a disposal with the same type usually is simple. A new sink is a bigger scope.

Keeping a disposal healthy

Run cold water while grinding and for a few seconds afterward. Avoid grease, coffee grounds, pasta and fibrous scraps like celery. These are the usual cause of kitchen drain trouble, covered on the faucet repair page when a sink issue involves the fixture and on the drain page when it involves the line. A few ice cubes and a citrus peel now and then keep the chamber clean.

The rules behind this kind of work come from the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00. Plumbers hold licenses issued by the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, so it is reasonable to ask for a license number and a written scope before any work begins.
