What a sewer camera inspection shows

The plumber feeds a waterproof camera head through a cleanout or a removed toilet and pushes it along the line toward the street. A monitor shows the live picture, and many systems record video and mark the distance. A transmitter in the head lets a locator find the pipe's depth and position from above ground.

You see the pipe wall, joints, roots, grease, sags that hold water, cracks, offsets and any connections. This is the evidence that turns a guess into a plan. It also lets you hear the plumber explain what each frame means, instead of just being handed an estimate.

Buying an older Winchester house? Inspect the sewer first

Winchester housing spans more than a century and a half, from mid 1800s homes near the center to the Wedgemere district built between roughly 1890 and 1920. A standard home inspection rarely covers the underground line. A camera check before closing can reveal root intrusion, a collapsed section or a pipe material that will need replacing soon.

If you are in an offer period, ask the seller for cleanout access and schedule the inspection quickly. Findings give you something to negotiate with, or the information to walk away. Repairs that appear later are your cost, so the inspection fee is small against the risk.

When to schedule an inspection

  • A drain that clogs repeatedly within weeks of cleaning
  • Slow drains in several fixtures at once
  • Sewage smell in the yard or basement
  • Lawn depressions or wet spots over the line
  • Before a major remodel that adds bathrooms
  • After a repair or lining, to confirm the result

For urgent backups, call the 24/7 line first. A drain that is stopped completely should be cleared before the camera can pass. The sewer line cleaning page covers that step.

Locating, depth and mapping

Many older properties have no plan showing where the sewer runs. The camera's transmitter can be tracked from the surface, so the plumber marks the line and its depth with paint or flags. That is vital before landscaping, a new fence or an addition, and it shows where to dig if a repair is needed.

Ask for the footage and the marked distances in writing. If a repair is recommended, the same record lets you get a second quote from another licensed plumber without paying for another inspection.

Software and reports

Some inspection systems use software that tags defects automatically. These tools are helpful for spotting patterns, but a person still has to read the footage, because pipe features like cleanout fittings and lateral connections are often mislabeled by algorithms. Ask who reviewed the video and signed the report.

What happens after the inspection

If the pipe is clean, you get a baseline and can stop worrying. If roots or grease show up, hydro jetting is the next step. If the camera finds a break, the options are on the sewer line repair page, and the footage is your tool for comparing quotes. Buyers looking at older houses in Arlington, MA can book the same inspection before closing.

The rules behind this kind of work come from the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00. Plumbers hold licenses issued by the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, so it is reasonable to ask for a license number and a written scope before any work begins.
