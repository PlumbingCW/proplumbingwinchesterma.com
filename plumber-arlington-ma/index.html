Winter in Arlington: frozen pipes and burst pipes

Cold snaps cause most of the emergencies on this page. Arlington's many two and three family houses have exterior walls, unheated porches and shared stacks, and a pipe that freezes in one unit can flood the one below. The first step when a pipe splits is the main valve, then the 24/7 line. The burst pipe repair page walks through shutoff, thawing and repair.

Prevention is cheaper than repair. Keep the heat at 55 degrees or higher when you travel, let a faucet drip on the coldest nights and open the cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls. In multi-family buildings, agree with the other unit on who checks the building when the temperature drops.

If your building has had more than one burst in a few years, the lines may be near the end of their life. A planned repiping project during a remodel costs less than repeated emergency repairs.

Spring thaw, Mill Brook and wet basements

Mill Brook runs through town and Spy Pond lies within its borders. After snowmelt and spring storms, basements near both can take on water, and the town's dense layout leaves little yard for drainage. A working sump pump and a clear discharge path make the difference, and the sump pump page covers testing, backups and where discharge may go.

Arlington owns and maintains the Great Meadow, which sits just across the border in Lexington. It is a reminder that this part of the valley is wet by nature. If your basement floor stays damp in April, have the cause identified before you finish the space. The cheaper fixes are outside, such as gutters and grading.

Summer and fall: drains, roots and old stacks

Arlington housing has long been a mix of double and triple deckers and smaller single-family homes, built in waves from the early 1900s on. Stacks that serve several floors collect grease, wipes and scale, and roots enter old laterals under street trees. When a kitchen sink on an upper floor backs up, the blockage might be below, so ask the plumber to trace the stack.

Start with drain cleaning for a single fixture. If the backup affects the lowest unit first, ask for a camera pass to look at the stack and the lateral. Shared lines are a team problem in multi-family houses, and a written finding helps everyone agree on the fix.

Arlington Center, East Arlington and Arlington Heights

Arlington Center holds the historic town hall and Robbins Library. East Arlington sits east of Franklin Street, and Arlington Heights covers the western side of town. The Minuteman Bikeway runs through the middle on the old rail bed. ZIP 02474 includes East Arlington and ZIP 02476 covers the rest, so give both the street and the ZIP when you call.
