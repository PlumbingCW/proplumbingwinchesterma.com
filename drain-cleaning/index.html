When a clogged drain is an emergency

A single slow bathroom sink can usually wait for a daytime appointment. A drain that has stopped completely is different, and so is any backup that is spreading. These are the signs that should send you to the phone right away:

  • Water or sewage coming up through a floor drain, tub or shower when you flush a toilet or run the washer
  • Two or more fixtures on the same level draining slowly or gurgling at once
  • A kitchen sink that fills when the dishwasher drains and does not clear after you stop
  • A sewage smell in the basement that was not there yesterday

Until the plumber arrives, stop using water, close the toilet lids and move rugs and stored boxes away from basement floor drains. Chemical drain openers do more harm than good in a fully blocked line, because the liquid sits in the pipe and can burn skin when the plumber opens the trap.

What blocks drains in Winchester homes

Much of Winchester was built out in layers. The town center has Greek Revival and Italianate houses from the mid 1800s, the Firth and Wildwood areas filled in with Queen Anne and Shingle style homes from the 1870s on, and the Wedgemere Historic District added large houses between roughly 1890 and 1920. Many of these homes were renovated, but some still carry original cast iron or clay drain lines.

Old iron develops a rough, scaled interior that catches grease, paper and hair, and roots find the joints in clay. So a clog in an older Winchester house is often the visible end of a rough pipe, not a one-time accident. That is why we clear the line first and recommend a camera look if the same drain stops again within a few weeks. See the sewer camera inspection page for how that works.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Grease cools inside the pipe and builds a waxy lining, coffee grounds settle in the bends, and a garbage disposal pushes fine food waste deeper into the line. The result is a drain that works for a minute and then slows to a crawl.

A hand auger or a small drum machine usually clears a kitchen branch. If the line goes back to a main that serves the whole house, the plumber may use a longer cable. Ask whether the disposal was removed to reach the clog and whether it was tested afterward. If the disposal itself is jammed or leaking, the garbage disposal repair page covers that side.

Snaking or hydro jetting: how the choice is made

A drain snake (cable) is the right tool when

  • The clog is a single blockage such as hair, a toy or a wad of paper
  • The line is a small branch from one fixture
  • You need the drain open today and the pipe is in decent condition

Hydro jetting is the better tool when

  • The drain clogs again within weeks after snaking
  • Grease or scale has narrowed the pipe wall to wall
  • Roots are present and the cable only punches a small hole through them

A cable makes a hole in a clog. A jet scrubs the pipe wall. Both have a place, and a good plumber explains why one is chosen over the other. If a snake got stuck, or the clog came back quickly, read about hydro jetting in Winchester before you agree to a third snaking visit.

Tub, shower and floor drains

Tub and shower drains clog with hair and soap film, and they sit low in the system where problems show up first. A plumber can often clear a tub drain through the overflow or the trap, with no tile removed. A floor drain in a basement is a different story. It is the lowest opening in the house, so a main line problem shows up there before anywhere else.

If water appears at a basement floor drain while upstairs fixtures still seem normal, treat it as a main line warning. Call the same day. A backup that reaches finished flooring or stored belongings becomes a sewer line cleaning job and a cleanup job together. The same drain service reaches Medford, where shared stacks in multi-family buildings can make one clog a neighbor's problem too.

Drain line leaks and slow recurring clogs

Sometimes the drain is not blocked at all. A cracked or separated drain line leaks into the wall or floor and also lets debris collect where the pipe sags. Signs include a damp spot below a bathroom, a musty cabinet floor or a drain that always seems a little slow. A camera and a dye test narrow the cause before anyone opens a wall.

Landlords, tenants and property managers

If you are calling for a rental, tell the dispatcher at the start that you are the owner or manager and who will be at the unit. Give the unit number, a tenant contact and whether the building shares a stack. Shared stacks in older multi-family homes mean one clog can show up in a neighbor's unit, so say which floors are affected.
