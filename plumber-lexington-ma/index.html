Lexington at a glance

Lexington is a Middlesex County town of about 34,500 residents at the 2020 census, roughly ten miles from downtown Boston. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for the whole town, and the primary ZIP code is 02421.

  • Distance from our Winchester address: about 5 miles
  • Villages: Lexington Centre, East Lexington, Munroe
  • Water: Quabbin Reservoir supply delivered through the MWRA system

Lexington Centre and the historic district

Lexington Centre surrounds the Battle Green, where the first shots of the Revolutionary War were fired on April 19, 1775. The Old Belfry, Buckman Tavern and the Hancock-Clarke House sit within a short walk, and shops and restaurants line the streets nearby. Buildings here are old, and old buildings have old pipes.

If you own or manage a property in the center, the practical point is access. Historic buildings can have thick plaster, tight cellars and limited shutoff access. Tell the dispatcher the building age and where the main valve is, if you know. The leak detection page explains how a plumber can find a hidden leak without opening more wall than needed.

East Lexington and the Great Meadow

East Lexington, known as the East Village, holds the Great Meadow, a wet meadow and marsh area owned by Arlington. Low land near it and near Vine Brook tends to hold water. Homes at the lower edge of the neighborhood benefit from a sump pump with a battery backup. Arlington, just to the south, shares that wet geography.

For a basement that stays damp, start outside with gutters and grading. If that is not enough, the basement waterproofing page compares interior drains and exterior systems and explains how to judge a quote.

Munroe, Peacock Farm and the mid-century subdivisions

Lexington grew from farmland to dense subdivisions by the 1970s, and mid-century neighborhoods like Peacock Farm, built between 1952 and 1958, and Six Moon Hill reflect that era. Homes of that period commonly use copper supply lines and a mix of cast iron and later PVC drains. They are younger than the center, and their problems are different: pinhole leaks, aging water heaters and original fixtures at the end of their life.

If your house is in this age range, a tankless water heater or a heat pump unit is a common upgrade. The water heater installation page covers venting and electrical needs, and boiler repair covers the heating side when a boiler feeds the house.

Remodels, additions and larger homes

Additions and kitchen and bath remodels bring new plumbing to houses of any age, and the permit and inspection steps matter. The plumbing installation page explains rough-in, vents and the inspection sequence. Ask the Lexington building department which permits apply to your project.

Water arrives from the Quabbin Reservoir by way of the Wachusett Reservoir and MWRA tunnels, and enters Lexington at four metered connection points. Lexington's Water and Sewer Division can answer questions about pressure and water quality on your street. The report we reviewed does not list hardness, so we do not quote a figure.
