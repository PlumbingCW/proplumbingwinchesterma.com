Boiler emergency: no heat or a leaking boiler

Winters in Massachusetts are long, and a heating failure is not a comfort problem, it is a safety one. Pipes can freeze in a house with no heat, and a leaking boiler can release a lot of water and cause damage. Work through this list while you wait:

  1. If you smell gas or see soot, leave and call your gas utility or 911
  2. Switch off the boiler at its emergency switch if it is leaking or making unusual noise
  3. Close the feed valve to the boiler if water is running from the unit
  4. Check the thermostat, the power switch and the pressure gauge, and write down the reading
  5. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see

Keep faucets dripping and open cabinet doors on exterior walls if the house is cooling, to guard against frozen pipes. The burst pipe repair page explains what to do if a pipe splits.

Hot water boilers in older Winchester houses

Many Winchester houses from the mid 1800s through the early 1900s were built with steam or gravity hot water heat and later converted. Radiators, cast iron pipe, zone valves and an expansion tank are the classic pieces. The age of the system matters, because old fittings and iron radiators can leak at joints and valves after decades of cycling.

A newer house may have baseboard hot water in zones with a circulator pump per zone. These systems fail at pumps, zone valves, air in the lines and low pressure. The plumbing side of the system, which is the water, the pipes, the valves and the pumps, is what this page covers.

Leaks: what the plumber checks first

A boiler that needs frequent water added is losing it somewhere. The plumber checks the relief valve, the circulator flanges, the air vent, the expansion tank, radiator valves and the pipe runs. A leak inside the boiler jacket usually means the heat exchanger has failed, and then replacement is the question.

Do not keep refilling a boiler to mask a leak. New water brings oxygen and minerals that corrode the system. A boiler with a slow leak and a pressure that creeps up and down is telling you something. A leak under a floor can be traced with a leak detection visit.

What a plumber covers and what needs a heating technician

Plumbers handle boiler piping, relief valves, expansion tanks, circulators, zone valves, feed valves and water leaks. Burner and combustion problems on oil or gas boilers may need a technician licensed for that work. Tell the dispatcher what you know about the fuel and the symptoms, so the right person is sent. Maintenance that prevents winter failures

Have the system checked before the heating season. Watch the pressure gauge, which usually sits near 12 to 15 psi cold on a residential hot water system, and write down the reading so you can spot changes. Bleed radiators if they gurgle and keep furniture and drapes clear of them. Old boiler rooms deserve a carbon monoxide detector.

The rules behind this kind of work come from the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00. Plumbers hold licenses issued by the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, so it is reasonable to ask for a license number and a written scope before any work begins.
