Faucet emergency: valves that will not close

Three situations are urgent. A shutoff valve that will not turn, a supply line that has burst or split and a shower or tub valve that pours water inside the wall. In each case the first move is the nearest shutoff, which is the oval or round valve under a sink or behind a toilet, and then the main if that fails.

Do not force a stuck valve with a pipe wrench, because an old stop can break and turn a drip into a flood. Instead, close the main shutoff for the house and call. The plumber can replace the failed stop with a quarter-turn valve, which is more reliable and easier to use next time.

Why faucets drip: parts inside the handle

Most drips come from a worn washer, O-ring, seat or cartridge inside the faucet. Compression faucets with two handles use rubber washers that harden. Cartridge faucets use a replaceable insert, and ball and ceramic disc types have their own parts. The repair is usually a part swap, if parts are available.

For older faucets, parts may no longer exist, so replacement is the sensible path. A drip is not only noise. A faucet that leaks one drop per second can waste thousands of gallons per year, so a fix usually pays for itself on the water bill.

Single-handle faucet leaks

Single-handle faucets often drip from the spout or leak around the base of the handle. A drip at the spout points to the cartridge or the seats and springs. A leak at the base points to worn O-rings. A plumber identifies the make, finds the right cartridge and installs it with new O-rings, then tests the flow and the shutoff.

If you can see a model number or brand name on the faucet, tell the dispatcher. A common cartridge is often on the truck, while a specialty one may need an order. Mention if the faucet is in a rental so the invoice can be sent to the owner. Landlords in the Medford service area use the same process for unit calls.

Shower valves, tub spouts and showerheads

Shower and tub fixtures hide their valves behind the wall. A leak that shows in the ceiling below a bathroom, a handle that will not hold temperature or a diverter that sends water to both the spout and the showerhead all point to the valve. Repair means swapping the cartridge, which is often possible from the front through the trim.

If the valve body is corroded or no parts exist, replacement means opening the wall. Many older Winchester bathrooms have tile over plaster, and it can be worth having the plumber discuss access options, including from an adjoining closet or the back of the wall. If a leak has already stained the ceiling, see leak detection.

Sink supply lines and shutoffs

Braided supply lines last years but not forever. A bulge or rust at the fitting is a sign it is due. Replacing the line and a stuck stop valve takes minutes and prevents a flood under a vanity. Do the same for the washer and ice maker lines, which are common sources of water damage.

Kitchen sink faucet changes often go along with a new disposal or a sink replacement. The garbage disposal repair and installation page covers that piece.

Repair or replace the faucet?

A fixture older than fifteen years, with a corroded body or no parts available, is a replace candidate. A newer faucet with a worn cartridge is a repair. Plumbing fixtures on a Winchester remodel are often part of a larger plan, and plumbing installation covers new fixtures, vanities and kitchen sinks.

What to do before the plumber arrives at a leaking faucet

  • Close the small shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise
  • Open the faucet briefly to relieve pressure and confirm the flow has stopped
  • Dry the cabinet and set a towel or tray under the supply lines
  • Do not force a stuck valve, because a snapped stem makes the leak worse
  • Take a photo of the faucet label or the brand name on the handle

In Massachusetts, the state plumbing code at 248 CMR 10.00 sets the rules for installation and repair, and licensing comes from the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Winchester Building Department or your plumber what applies to yours.
